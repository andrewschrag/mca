Wave 1: the founding team

The roles the December team decision picks from. The nine-month roadmap assumes the smallest team that works; each hire here takes a lane off it. Three permanent seats decided in December, one on a trigger in the spring, and the platform engineering bought from the host.

Draft rev 4 · 19 October 2026Decision: mid-December, with phase 1 fundingDefault: lean through March; January checkpoint

Why these roles

Hire for what has to survive a vendor change, what sits on the first horizon's critical path, and what the host is not already building.

The third test is new. The host institution is building a knowledge graph over a lakehouse for its own reasons, going live this November, with a research informatics team that works on project funding. MCA and the host have agreed to build one path. That moves the environment, the lakehouse, ingestion and deployment to the host's side of the line, bought and scoped rather than hired.

What stays on MCA's side is narrower and sharper: the CHD knowledge graph's content, the extraction implementation and its validation, the concept catalogue and the methods that make the data usable, and the agreements and rules that are MCA's half of the host model. Each hire will be the only person in their discipline for at least six months, so each has to do the work, not only manage it.

Work that meets all three tests
  1. 01The CHD knowledge graph's content and the extraction pipeline that reads notes into it
  2. 02The validation evidence, methods and concept catalogue that make the data usable, starting with the first device study's protocol
  3. 03The agreements, awards and charters that put the platform inside a host institution and set its rules
  4. 04Proof of dynamic assembly on real questions, before outside researchers depend on it

The roles

Every seat is permanent. Three report to the Head of Patient Data Strategy; the analyst reports to the Director of Epidemiology & Evidence

Evidence · first hire · deputy to the Head

Director of Epidemiology & Evidence

Leads the science of the platform: how its data becomes evidence anyone can trust.

Type
Permanent
Owns
Validation protocol, methods, consultation and review, FDA, the concept catalogue
Reports
Clinical Data Analyst, then the central methods group
Start
Q1 2027; a senior methodologist can be contracted for the protocol and converted

The scientific lead of the evidence function and the Head of Patient Data Strategy's deputy. First hire, because the first device post-approval study is weeks away and its validation protocol is a first-quarter deliverable that goes to FDA through the monthly cadence and settles when to bring in a second reviewing office. Four jobs. First, methods for the platform: how cohorts, time-zero, follow-up and missingness work when data comes from a graph rather than a registry form. Second, MCA's own studies, starting with the paired validation study in which every value a trial abstracts by hand is also abstracted by the AI, blind, with discordances adjudicated. Third, the consultation and review service for groups using the platform. Fourth, FDA. Owns the concept catalogue.

  • Owns the catalogueWhat concepts exist, how they are defined, versioning and citation rules, and what goes to the Data Standards committee for clinical sign-off.
  • Runs analytics the decentralized wayAnalysis happens at the centres and the cores. The central group sets methods, unblocks and trains, and reviews; it does not do the field's analysis for it.
  • One voice to FDAMCA owns the validation design and the evidence that platform data is fit for purpose. The trials partner owns trial conduct. The host provides review. The lines are drawn before the first protocol.
First-quarter deliverables

The validation protocol for the first device study, reviewed with FDA by about March. The health-economics and access questions being added to the device case report forms, which are a study-design decision. The first concept definitions the extraction build needs, so the catalogue exists before the pipeline does.

One scope question the December advisor decision settles: if an advisor continues in the role of leading how analysts across the centres work together, this seat narrows to methods, validation, FDA and the catalogue. If not, it carries both.

Must have

  • Real-world evidence methodsDoctoral-level epidemiology or biostatistics: cohort and post-approval study design, external controls, natural-history comparators, target trial emulation.
  • Computable phenotyping on EHR dataHas written and versioned phenotype definitions with temporal logic against real records, and knows how EHR data misleads.
  • Methods leadership across investigatorsHas run a consult or review service, set standards for analysis plans, and raised the quality of other people's studies without owning them.
  • Validation statisticsAgreement measures, sensitivity and PPV for each variable, adjudication design, and sizing a validation sample.
  • Regulatory experienceFitness-for-purpose under FDA's real-world evidence guidance, including the 2025 device guidance; pre-submissions and documents a reviewer will act on; explains technical work plainly to non-technical reviewers.
  • Builds a teamStandards for reproducible code (R, Python or SQL), and the judgment to grow a central group that trains rather than a central group that does everything.

Gives an edge

  • A clinician-epidemiologist with CHD depth, who covers definitions and clinical judgment in one person
  • Multi-stakeholder RWE pilots (Friends of Cancer Research), an RWE company (Flatiron, Aetion), or FDA's Sentinel or NEST programs
  • Device post-approval studies built on registry data
  • Published validations of ML-derived variables
  • Small-population and rare-disease methods

Rules them out

  • A methodologist whose work no regulator or outside investigator has ever relied on
  • A regulatory affairs specialist who can't design the statistics
  • A trial statistician who has not worked with observational EHR data
Interview test

Two parts. Sketch the sample size and acceptance criteria for an AI-versus-human abstraction comparison on a 60-patient device cohort with ten-year follow-up, and explain how they would present it to FDA. Then take a study request from an outside investigator and show how they would review it: what they would push back on, what concept definitions it needs, and what they would approve as is.

Technical core · second hire

ML / Clinical NLP Scientist

Builds the reader that turns notes into the graph. MCA's one technical seat in the first horizon.

Type
Permanent
Owns
Extraction, evaluation, confidence scoring, the AI position, MCA's side of the shared build
Start
February, for the build start

Builds the graph-guided extraction pipeline that turns clinical notes into assertions in the knowledge graph, running on off-the-shelf models, inside the host's environment and on the host's lakehouse. Owns how good the extraction is and how sure it is: per-concept accuracy, confidence scores, and the learning loop from the two gold standards (registry submissions and trial hand-abstraction). With the host building the platform layer, this is MCA's one technical seat: the person who holds MCA's side of the shared build and makes sure the derived layer, the graph content and the pipeline code are MCA's, documented and exportable. A scientist who ships production code, not an analyst.

  • The AI position for hospitalsAcademic centres are adding AI clauses to data use agreements now. Someone has to answer a hospital's data-use lawyer in technical terms: models run inside the host enclave, no training on hospital data, redaction where a site requires it.
  • Vendor evaluation and the bake-offExtraction models, including the vendors the incumbent registries are trialling, judged on an independent validation set. MCA's side of the host's ingestion bake-off: notes upload, patient-authorized FHIR, bulk FHIR and the EHR-native routes.
  • The build and the first 25 conceptsThe graph and extraction build from February, with 25 concepts validated against the gold standard by about June, including the ones that disappoint.

Must have

  • LLM extraction from clinical textRetrieval design, structured output, and prompt and pipeline engineering on unstructured notes.
  • Graph-guided retrievalGraphRAG or close equivalents, using domain knowledge to steer what the model reads and asserts.
  • Evaluation disciplineBuilds gold-standard sets, measures precision and recall per concept, and re-tests whenever a model changes. Evaluation sets are a joint deliverable with the Director of Epidemiology & Evidence, so clinical content is never decided by whoever writes the prompts.
  • Confidence scoring and calibrationScores that mean something, so human review can scale with confidence and project need.
  • Working inside someone else's environmentDeploys into a hospital's enclave under its security rules, with de-identification and redaction tooling where required, and can say plainly what the models do and do not see.
  • Production-quality, portable codeModel-independent, so a better model replaces the current one without rework; documented so the pipeline can move if the host relationship ever ends.

Gives an edge

  • Has moved a team from human abstraction to ML extraction; oncology RWE (Flatiron, Tempus and similar) is the closest precedent
  • Has published or shipped validated ML-derived clinical variables
  • Has worked with clinical terminologies (SNOMED CT, HPO) in an extraction pipeline
  • Has built on a lakehouse with a knowledge graph or ontology layer over it
  • Pediatric or rare-disease records, where examples are scarce

Rules them out

  • A data analyst whose output is notebooks rather than pipelines
  • A researcher who has never run a model against messy clinical data in production
  • Anyone who cannot explain to a privacy officer where the data goes
Interview test

Give them a dozen de-identified notes and three target concepts. Ask how they would build the first evaluation set, what score would mean "ready for a human to review less," and how they would know when a model change made things worse. Then ask them to write, in a paragraph, the AI position they would give a hospital's counsel.

Network · gated on the January checkpoint

Director of Network Operations

Runs MCA's half of the host model: the agreements, the awards and the rules.

Type
Permanent
Owns
Term sheet to signed agreement, governance charter, the cohort RFA, the access function
Holds
The cohort and cores budget and the agreement pipeline
Start
March or April, ahead of the letter of intent; later if January is green

In the host model the institution holds the data and MCA holds the contracting and the governance. That makes the agreements and the rules MCA's one irreplaceable asset, and someone has to run them as a job. The role is defined by outcomes, not coordination. Its first-horizon content is smaller than first drawn: the network data use agreement is re-papered by the host and its counsel as a name change plus a letter to sites, and recruiting hospitals for feeds is physician-led, co-led with each site's registry lead. What remains is what nobody else holds, and it peaks from April, when agreement negotiation, the governance charter and the RFA run at once.

  • SignedThe term sheet (about March), the letter of intent (about April) and the host agreement itself (about six months later), with the four protective terms: a license to the derived layer, a priced right to migrate, no exclusivity, and consent that can be reassigned. Runs the issue list with outside counsel and the counterparty's pace.
  • AwardedThe RFA to groups already running consented cohorts, awards about August: published criteria, a real competition, IP assigned or licensed at award, milestone payments, a named MCA counterpart. The first award is read by the field as the policy. Cores are not defined up front; the ones that already exist come first.
  • CharteredThe steering committee with seats for patients and families, sites, registries, the host and MCA, seated about June and required before any dataset is released; the Data Standards committee; the independent patient coalition. Later, the access function that applies the rules per request.

Must have

  • Has signed thingsHealth-system research agreements: DUAs, BAAs, master research agreements and reliance agreements (for example SMART IRB). Has carried an institutional agreement from issue list to signature with outside counsel, and works fluently with IRB, tech transfer and compliance offices at children's hospitals.
  • Has awarded thingsHas run a competitive funding call end to end: criteria, review, award terms including IP, milestone payments and counterpart management.
  • Has chartered thingsHas stood up a governance body or network committee from nothing: charter, seats, decision rules, and the first year of operation.
  • Multi-site network operationsSite onboarding and coordinating-center workflows in a research network.
  • Credibility and diplomacyCredible with academic clinicians and hospital executives, and diplomatic enough with registries that "partner, not replacement" holds up in practice.

Gives an edge

  • A disease foundation that ran a data network (for example CF Foundation, Michael J. Fox Foundation, ASH Research Collaborative)
  • A rare-disease or national research network coordinating center, or an NIH program officer background
  • Has administered a research network's data access committee

Rules them out

  • A program-management office background: trackers, status reports and alignment meetings with no agreement, award or charter they can point to
  • A business-development hunter who treats partners as sales targets
  • Anyone who describes the job as coordinating other people's work
Interview test

Ask for the last three things they got signed, awarded or chartered, and what went wrong on each. Then walk them through the open legal issues for a host institution agreement and ask which three they would settle first, and with whom. Someone who answers the first question with process rather than outcomes is the wrong hire.

Why this is not a program director, and not a product hybrid

The familiar failure of a program director is a role defined by activity: cadence, logs and alignment, with no domain, no budget and no outcome of its own. The work routes around it and the leader gains a layer instead of time. This seat is drawn the other way: it owns a budget, a pipeline and three outcomes, and reports on those rather than on status. Cut anything from this description that reads as coordination.

A hybrid with product was considered and set aside for the first horizon. The Head of Patient Data Strategy is the product lead while the product is the strategy itself, and agreements and product are different muscles. The product role that does make sense is a technical product lead for the workspaces, in the second horizon when vendors start building (see Roles on a trigger).

The partnerships title also waits. Two open decisions would take that half of the job: the clinical lead, who co-owns the hospital lane whichever form the role takes, and whether MCA creates a wider partnerships role that owns each institution's top-level relationship across all of MCA's work. Either way, this seat builds the institution-by-institution relationship map and agreement register so they can become MCA-wide.

Evidence · on trigger

Clinical Data Analyst

Turns real questions into datasets and results, and proves dynamic assembly works.

Type
Permanent
Reports to
Director of Epidemiology & Evidence
Owns
Study datasets, analyses, catalogue maintenance, the use-case feedback loop
Start
About May, when the first real data lands

Takes a study question, writes its dataset definition, assembles it from versioned concepts, checks it and runs the analysis. This is the platform's first user, and the person who shows dynamic assembly working on real questions before outside researchers depend on it. Maintains the concept catalogue as code. Sits between the use cases, epidemiology and engineering, with the statistics and the engineering habits to work in all three.

  • Proves the differentiatorPhase 1 has to demonstrate dynamic assembly, not only extraction. The first dataset by dynamic assembly lands about September. This role is the demonstration.
  • Closes the loopSees first when a concept is missing or extraction is weak on the variable that matters, and sends it back as a specific, testable gap.
  • Tests the workspacesWrites the user-side acceptance tests for vendor-built workspaces before anyone outside uses them.
The trigger, now with dates

Raw notes from the trials partner's sites about March. First consented study patients about April. Twenty-five concepts validated about June. The analyst lands when the first of these is real, a few months after the Director, and is in place before paired abstraction begins.

Must have

  • Epidemiology and biostatistics in practiceCohort definitions, time-zero, censoring, survival analysis, propensity methods, external controls.
  • Engineering habitsPython or R plus SQL, version control, tested and reproducible pipelines, and cohort logic written as code rather than spreadsheet steps.
  • Clinical data literacyKnows the ways EHR data misleads, knows congenital heart disease well enough to sanity-check a cohort, and talks to clinicians without a translator.
  • Data quality instinctProfiles completeness and plausibility before trusting a dataset, and records every assumption.
  • Clear reportingTables and figures an investigator or reviewer can act on.

Gives an edge

  • A quantitative sciences team at an RWE company (Flatiron, Aetion and similar)
  • Has validated abstracted or ML-derived variables
  • Pediatric or rare-disease cohorts
  • Has contributed to analyses submitted to FDA

Rules them out

  • A statistician who can't work in the pipeline
  • A data engineer without study-design judgment
  • A dashboard-only BI analyst
Interview test

Give them a question (late arrhythmia after Fontan, by era of surgery) and a small synthetic dataset. Ask for the cohort definition, time-zero and the variables needed, what they would check before trusting the data, and which concepts they would need defined and signed off before they could proceed.

Analytics grows the decentralized way: analysts at the centres and the cores do the field's work, and the central group stays small, a second technician when a second funded use case has its own study calendar. The central group unblocks, trains and reviews.

The concept catalogue

Key to the platform, so it gets a named owner, a named maintainer and a named clinical check from day one

Director of Epidemiology & EvidenceOwns it: what concepts exist, how they are defined, versioning and citation, what goes to the committee.
Clinical Data AnalystMaintains it as code and builds study-specific logic on top of the reusable definitions.
ML / Clinical NLP ScientistConsumes it for extraction, carries the technical terminology mapping, and reports where a concept cannot yet be delivered.
Clinical lead and the Data Standards committeeSign off that definitions are clinically sound, adjudicate discordances, and endorse new variable definitions and novel abstraction.

A standalone clinical informatics seat was considered and set aside for wave 1. Computable definitions are epidemiology, terminology mapping is bounded and shared with the ML scientist and a terminology service, and clinical judgment comes from the clinical lead and registry clinicians. The seat returns on a trigger (below). Two safeguards: seat the Data Standards committee early, so clinical sign-off never depends on one person, and make the ML scientist's evaluation sets a joint deliverable with the Director.

Roles on a trigger

Hired when a condition is met, not on a date

  • Platform Data EngineerMoved off wave 1 on 19 OctoberGraph storage, lineage, the assembly engine and deployment were MCA's seat until the host turned out to be building the same platform layer and agreed to one path. In the first horizon that engineering is bought from the host and the ML scientist holds MCA's side. Trigger: the host's capacity cannot take the work, the migration right has to be exercised rather than held, or hospital feeds start at scale (about the end of 2027). Mitigation until then: the term sheet makes the derived layer and the pipeline code MCA's, documented and exportable.
  • Patient & Family Engagement LeadFractional or partner-led in the first horizonOwns consent design, the patient and family seats and the independent coalition's charter, and the patient-return concept. Trigger: patient-authorized records for study patients start (about June), or the coalition charter, whichever comes first.
  • Clinical Informatics LeadSet aside for wave 1Owns terminology engineering and the catalogue's day-to-day curation. Trigger: the catalogue's terminology work exceeds what the Director and analyst can carry alongside studies, or registry ingestion begins and definitions must reconcile across sources. Expected in the second horizon.
  • Technical product leadSecond horizon, when vendors start buildingOwns the workspace specs, vendor evaluation and acceptance, and user research across the six personas, patients and families first. Until then the Head is the product lead and the analyst writes the user-side acceptance tests. Trigger: the first workspace vendor is engaged.
  • Data governance and access leadSecond horizonRuns the access function day to day: the rulebook, decisions on requests the rules don't settle within the published turnaround, and support for the steering committee. Until request volume justifies it, the Director of Network Operations holds the function.

Order of hiring

Epidemiology & Evidence first. The ML scientist second, in place for the February build start. Network Operations in March or April ahead of the letter of intent, or later if January is green. The analyst on its trigger about May.

The Director is first because the validation protocol is a first-quarter deliverable and the catalogue has to exist before the pipeline does; a senior methodologist can be contracted for the protocol and converted. The scientist is second because the graph and extraction build starts in February on the host's new platform, and the AI position for hospitals needs a technical owner before the first hospital asks. Network Operations is gated because the host and its counsel carry the early agreement work; the seat earns its place when negotiation, charter and RFA overlap in the spring.

The cautionThe roadmap's durations already assume the lean team. Every quarter a hire slips is a quarter the Head carries that lane. The January checkpoint should ask, lane by lane, which items are behind because of capacity rather than counterparties, and move the hire whose lane is red.
