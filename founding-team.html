Why these roles

Hire for work that has to survive a vendor change and sits on the first phase's critical path.

MCA builds the platform on an out-of-the-box stack (Databricks or similar). The build starts in an MCA sandbox with no patient data and moves inside the anchor institution once the agreement is signed. The anchor institution supplies the environment, its FHIR feed and security. What MCA builds stays MCA's, documented and portable.

Each hire will be the only person in their discipline for at least six months and has to do the work, not just manage it.

MCA's work
  1. 01The platform foundation, the CHD knowledge graph and the extraction pipeline
  2. 02Validation, methods and the concept catalogue, starting with the first use case
  3. 03The agreements, awards and charters that put the platform inside the anchor institution and set its rules
  4. 04Dynamic assembly working on real questions before outside researchers rely on it

The roles

All permanent. Four report to the Head of Patient Data Strategy; the analyst reports to the Senior Director of Epidemiology & Evidence.

Evidence · first hire · deputy

Senior Director of Epidemiology & Evidence

Leads the science of the platform and deputizes for the Head.

Type
Permanent
Owns
Methods, validation, FDA, consultation and review, concept catalogue
Manages
Clinical Data Analyst
Start
First offer, recruited directly through the Head's network

First hire because the role helps guide the decisions that follow, and because the first use case's validation design goes to FDA early. Sets the platform's methods: how cohorts, time-zero, follow-up and missingness work when data comes from a graph rather than a registry form. Leads MCA's own studies, starting with paired validation, where every value abstracted by hand is also abstracted by the AI, blind, with discordances adjudicated. Runs consultation and review for groups using the platform, and the FDA relationship.

  • Concept catalogueDecides which concepts exist, how they're defined, versioned and cited, and what goes to the Data Standards committee.
  • Distributed analyticsAnalysts at the centres and cores do the analysis. The central group sets methods, trains and reviews.
  • FDAMCA owns the validation design and fitness-for-purpose evidence. The trials partner owns trial conduct; the anchor institution provides review.
Early work

Validation design for the first use case, reviewed with FDA. Health-economics and access questions for the device case report forms. The first concept set, before the extraction build needs it.

If an advisor keeps leading how analysts across the centres work together, this role narrows to methods, validation, FDA and the catalogue.

Must have

  • Real-world evidence methodsDoctoral-level epidemiology or biostatistics: cohort and post-approval study design, external controls, natural-history comparators, target trial emulation.
  • Computable phenotypingHas written and versioned phenotype definitions with temporal logic against real EHR data.
  • Methods leadershipHas run a consult or review service and set standards for other investigators' analysis plans.
  • Validation statisticsAgreement measures, per-variable sensitivity and PPV, adjudication design, validation sample size.
  • Regulatory experienceFDA real-world evidence guidance, including the 2025 device guidance; pre-submissions; explains technical work to non-technical reviewers.
  • Team buildingSets standards for reproducible code (R, Python or SQL) and grows a small central group.

Gives an edge

  • Clinician-epidemiologist with CHD depth
  • Friends of Cancer Research RWE pilots, an RWE company (Flatiron, Aetion), or FDA's Sentinel or NEST programs
  • Device post-approval studies on registry data
  • Published validations of ML-derived variables
  • Small-population and rare-disease methods

Rules them out

  • No work a regulator or outside investigator has relied on
  • Regulatory affairs without statistical design
  • Trial statistics only, no observational EHR work
Interview test

Sketch sample size and acceptance criteria for an AI-versus-human abstraction comparison on a 60-patient device cohort with ten-year follow-up, and how to present it to FDA. Then review a study request from an outside investigator: what to push back on, which concept definitions it needs, what to approve.

Technical core · early

ML / Clinical NLP Scientist

Builds the extraction pipeline that reads notes into the graph.

Type
Permanent
Owns
Extraction, evaluation, confidence scoring, AI position for hospitals
Start
With the sandbox build

Builds the graph-guided extraction pipeline on off-the-shelf models, first in MCA's sandbox and then inside the anchor institution. Owns accuracy and confidence per concept, and the learning loop from two gold standards: registry submissions and trial hand-abstraction. Works with the platform data engineer; whether the two are one role or two is open. Ships production code.

  • AI position for hospitalsAcademic centres are adding AI clauses to data use agreements. This role answers their counsel: models run inside the anchor institution's enclave, no training on hospital data, redaction where a site requires it.
  • Vendors and modelsEvaluates extraction vendors, including those the incumbent registries are trialling, on an independent validation set. Sets up model access in the sandbox.
  • MilestonesExtraction on de-identified notes, then a first pipeline, then the first 25 concepts validated.

Must have

  • LLM extraction from clinical textRetrieval design, structured output, prompt and pipeline engineering on unstructured notes.
  • Graph-guided retrievalGraphRAG or similar, using domain knowledge to steer what the model reads and asserts.
  • EvaluationGold-standard sets, per-concept precision and recall, regression testing on every model change. Evaluation sets are built jointly with the Senior Director of Epidemiology & Evidence.
  • Confidence calibrationScores reliable enough to set how much human review each use needs.
  • Working in a hospital enclaveDeploys under its security rules, with de-identification and redaction tooling, and can explain what the models see.
  • Portable production codeModel-independent and documented, so the pipeline can move if the anchor institution relationship ends.

Gives an edge

  • Moved a team from human abstraction to ML extraction (Flatiron, Tempus or similar)
  • Published or shipped validated ML-derived clinical variables
  • Clinical terminologies (SNOMED CT, HPO) in an extraction pipeline
  • A lakehouse with a knowledge graph or ontology layer
  • Pediatric or rare-disease records

Rules them out

  • Notebooks, not pipelines
  • No production experience with clinical data
  • Can't explain to a privacy officer where the data goes
Interview test

Give a dozen de-identified notes and three target concepts. Ask how they'd build the first evaluation set, what score would justify less human review, and how they'd catch a regression after a model change. Then have them write the AI position for a hospital's counsel in one paragraph.

Technical core · scope open

Platform Data Engineer

Builds the platform foundation and moves it inside the anchor institution.

Type
Permanent
Owns
Stack, knowledge graph, assembly engine, workspace, identity, release path
Start
Before the build moves inside the anchor institution
Open
Separate role, or combined with the ML scientist

Builds the knowledge graph, the three storage layers with lineage, and the assembly engine on the chosen stack. Starts in an MCA sandbox with synthetic records and de-identified gold-standard pairs, then migrates inside the anchor institution once the agreement is signed. Owns the workspace there, identity resolution and tokenization, and the release path. Writes the acceptance tests MCA holds vendors to.

  • SandboxStack stood up first, so the graph, assembly engine and first pipeline exist before patient data does.
  • Anchor institutionWorkspace inside its environment, the FHIR feed with notes landing in the raw layer, and MCA's derived layer kept exportable.
  • Identity and releaseTokenization provider, identity resolution, then de-identification and access tiers.
Open: one role or two

Combining this with the ML scientist costs less and fits a small team. But few people do both well, and the first phase needs both at full speed: sandbox, graph and workspace on one side; pipeline, evaluation and 25 concepts on the other. Decided with the team decision.

Must have

  • Graph data modelingIn production, with time and provenance: source, date and confidence on every assertion.
  • Healthcare data engineeringFHIR R4 and US Core, clinical documents, terminology services, HL7v2.
  • Lineage and storageImmutable raw landing, a versioned assertion layer, reproducible datasets.
  • Hospital environmentsHIPAA-grade security, audit logging, de-identification, a hospital's cloud tenant.
  • Identity and privacyPatient tokenization or privacy-preserving record linkage, tiered access.
  • Hands-on vendor managementWrites code daily; scopes statements of work, writes acceptance tests, directs contractors.

Gives an edge

  • Databricks or a similar lakehouse with a graph or ontology layer
  • Systems with audit trails a regulator examined
  • Academic medical center or research coordinating center experience

Rules them out

  • Hasn't coded in years
  • Defaults to a record-centric common data model
Interview test

How would you model one patient's history, from birth through Fontan completion to adult care, so a question nobody has asked yet can still be answered?

Network · early

Director of Network Operations

Runs MCA's agreements, awards and governance.

Type
Permanent
Owns
Anchor institution agreement, network DUA, hospital recruiting, governance charter, coalition, access function
Budget
Cohorts and cores; the agreement pipeline
Start
Early, to keep the anchor institution agreement on track

The anchor institution holds the data; MCA holds the contracts and the governance. This role runs both and is measured on what gets signed, awarded and chartered. The early months are the heaviest, while the anchor institution agreement is negotiated.

  • SignedThe anchor institution agreement, with four protective terms: license to the derived layer, a priced right to migrate, no exclusivity, reassignable consent. Then hospitals recruited for feeds, co-led with each registry lead, and the network data use agreement re-papered.
  • AwardedThe RFA to groups already running consented cohorts: published criteria, real competition, IP terms at award, milestone payments, a named MCA counterpart. Existing cores first.
  • CharteredThe governance charter, the patient and family coalition, and the steering committee, with seats for patients and families, sites, registries, the anchor institution and MCA. No dataset is released before it sits. Later, the access function.

Must have

  • AgreementsHas taken institutional agreements (DUAs, BAAs, master research agreements, SMART IRB reliance) from issue list to signature with outside counsel and children's hospital IRB, tech transfer and compliance offices.
  • Funding callsHas run a competitive call end to end: criteria, review, IP terms, milestones.
  • Governance bodiesHas stood up a committee from scratch and run its first year.
  • Network operationsSite onboarding and coordinating-center workflows.
  • CredibilityTrusted by academic clinicians and hospital executives, and by registries as a partner.

Gives an edge

  • A disease foundation that ran a data network (CF Foundation, Michael J. Fox Foundation, ASH Research Collaborative)
  • A rare-disease or national research network coordinating center, or NIH program officer
  • Ran a data access committee

Rules them out

  • Program management with no agreement, award or charter to show for it
  • Business development that treats partners as sales targets
Interview test

Ask for the last three things they got signed, awarded or chartered, and what went wrong on each. Then walk through the open legal issues for an anchor institution agreement and ask which three they'd settle first, and with whom.

Notes on scope

Not a program director. The role owns a budget, a pipeline and three outcomes, and reports on those.

No product hybrid in the first phase; the Head leads product. A technical product lead comes in the second phase, when vendors start building workspaces.

The partnerships title waits on two decisions: the clinical lead's role, and whether MCA creates a partnerships role across all its work. Either way, this role keeps the institution-by-institution relationship map and agreement register.

Clinical Data Analyst

Turns study questions into datasets and results.

Type
Permanent
Reports to
Senior Director of Epidemiology & Evidence
Owns
Study datasets, analyses, catalogue maintenance
Start
When real data arrives

Writes each study's dataset definition, assembles it from versioned concepts, checks it and runs the analysis. The platform's first user: shows dynamic assembly working before outside researchers rely on it. Maintains the concept catalogue as code.

  • Dynamic assemblyPhase 1 has to show it, not just extraction.
  • FeedbackReports missing concepts and weak extraction as specific, testable gaps.
  • Acceptance testsWrites the user-side tests for vendor-built workspaces.
When

The first of: the anchor institution's FHIR feed with notes, notes from the trials partner's sites, or the first consented records. In place before paired abstraction begins.

Must have

  • Applied epidemiologyCohort definitions, time-zero, censoring, survival analysis, propensity methods, external controls.
  • Engineering habitsPython or R and SQL, version control, tested and reproducible pipelines, cohort logic as code.
  • Clinical dataKnows EHR data's pitfalls and enough CHD to sanity-check a cohort.
  • Data qualityChecks completeness and plausibility first; documents assumptions.
  • ReportingTables and figures investigators and reviewers can use.

Gives an edge

  • Quantitative sciences at an RWE company (Flatiron, Aetion or similar)
  • Validated abstracted or ML-derived variables
  • Pediatric or rare-disease cohorts
  • Analyses submitted to FDA

Rules them out

  • Statistics without pipeline skills
  • Engineering without study design
  • Dashboards only
Interview test

Give a question (late arrhythmia after Fontan, by era of surgery) and a small synthetic dataset. Ask for the cohort definition, time-zero, variables, data checks, and which concepts need definitions before they start.

Analysis grows at the centres and cores. The central group stays small, adding a second analyst when a second funded use case has its own study calendar.

The concept catalogue

One owner, one maintainer, one clinical check.

Senior Director of Epidemiology & EvidenceOwns concepts, definitions, versioning and citation, and what goes to the committee.
Clinical Data AnalystMaintains it as code; builds study logic on the definitions.
ML / Clinical NLP ScientistUses it for extraction, maps terminologies, flags concepts extraction can't yet deliver.
Clinical lead and Data Standards committeeSign off definitions, adjudicate discordances, endorse new variables.

No separate clinical informatics role in wave 1. Definitions sit with epidemiology, terminology mapping with the ML scientist and a terminology service, and clinical judgment with the clinical lead and registry clinicians. Seat the Data Standards committee early so sign-off never depends on one person.

Bought, not hired

  • The stackDatabricks or similar.
  • Anchor institution research informaticsEnvironment, FHIR feed with notes, security and credentialing, scoped and costed up front. Their capacity is limited; the estimate shows what they add or drop.
  • Terminology servicesTerminology server and mapping tools, until the work needs a dedicated role.
  • Privacy and legalOutside counsel for the anchor institution agreement and AI position; fractional privacy support; redaction tooling where needed.
  • Commodity layers, including the user interfaceVendors, held to MCA's acceptance tests.

Roles on a trigger

Hired when a condition is met.

  • Patient & Family Engagement LeadFractional or partner-led in the first phaseConsent design, the coalition charter, the patient-return concept. Trigger: patient-authorized records start or the coalition charter, whichever is first.
  • Clinical Informatics LeadNot in wave 1Terminology engineering and day-to-day catalogue curation. Trigger: terminology work outgrows the Senior Director and analyst, or registry ingestion needs definitions reconciled across sources. Likely second phase.
  • Technical Product LeadSecond phaseWorkspace specs, vendor evaluation and acceptance, user research across the six personas, patients and families first. Trigger: the first workspace vendor is engaged.
  • Data Governance & Access LeadSecond phaseRuns access day to day: the rulebook, decisions on requests the rules don't cover, committee support. Until volume justifies it, the Director of Network Operations covers this.

Order of hiring

Epidemiology & Evidence first. Network Operations and the ML scientist next. Platform engineering before the build moves inside the anchor institution. The analyst when real data arrives.

The Senior Director guides the decisions that follow and owns the validation design that goes to FDA. Network Operations keeps the anchor institution agreement on track; everything inside the anchor institution waits on. The ML scientist starts the sandbox build and owns the AI position before hospitals ask. Platform engineering is needed when the build moves inside the anchor institution.

RiskThe roadmap assumes this team. Every month a hire slips, the Head carries that lane. At each checkpoint, check whether delays come from capacity or counterparties, and move up the hire whose lane is behind.
